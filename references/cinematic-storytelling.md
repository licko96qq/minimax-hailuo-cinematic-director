# Cinematic Storytelling

## Three-shot narrative contract

A valid three-shot Hailuo package must satisfy:

1. Setup
   Establish the world, scale, and visual promise.
2. Escalation
   Move closer to the dramatic center and introduce new information.
3. Payoff
   Deliver a stronger emotional or narrative conclusion than Shot 2.

If Shot 2 and Shot 3 both behave like escalation shots, the package is structurally weak.

## Continuity spine

Keep these stable across the sequence:

- same world
- same light logic
- same emotional objective
- same physical cause-and-effect chain

Continuity does not mean repeating the same composition.

## Difference matrix

Consecutive shots must differ on at least three major axes:

- camera height
- framing scale
- primary subject
- dramatic beat
- motion pattern
- human-presence level

Good example:

- Shot 1: high wide aerial world setup
- Shot 2: lower oblique tracking shot that discovers evidence
- Shot 3: distant payoff composition with a witness or final reveal

Bad example:

- Shot 1: high aerial river
- Shot 2: slightly different high aerial river
- Shot 3: another high aerial river

## Audit scoring rubric

Use this 100-point framing:

- Story structure: 25
- Continuity coherence: 20
- Shot distinctness: 30
- Prompt quality: 25

Treat `shot distinctness` as the hardest gate. A repetitive plan should fail even if the writing is pretty.

## Common failure modes

- every shot repeats the same composition
- the second shot introduces no new information
- the payoff is only a small variation of the escalation
- prompts use different words but describe the same cinematic image
- chained `i2v` anchors lock the sequence into visual sameness

## Fix strategy

When a package fails distinctness:

- lower or raise camera height sharply
- switch framing scale
- change the narrative subject
- add a new clue or human trace
- switch the payoff shot to `t2v` if anchor lock is too strong
